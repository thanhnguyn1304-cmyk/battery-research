# Replication Report: Methodology, Results, and Explanations

This report provides a comprehensive guide to understanding the methodology, steps, and results achieved during the replication of the paper: **"Machine learning-assisted structural parameters screening of Sb@C composites for high cycle capacity in sodium-ion battery"** (Elsevier 2025).

---

## 1. Overview of the Problem
The goal is to predict the **Remaining Capacity (RC)** of Sb@C composite electrodes in sodium-ion batteries after 100 cycles, using structural parameters.
The input dataset contains 90 samples collected from literature, with 7 features:
1. **AC** (Antimony Content): Ratio of Sb in the composite (ranges from 0.03 to 0.88).
2. **AS** (Antimony Size): Average size of Sb particles in nm (ranges from 1.78 to 400).
3. **RP** (Raman Peak ratio, $I_D/I_G$): Measure of carbon defects (ranges from 0.30 to 3.13).
4. **IS** (Interplanar Spacing) in nm: Structural spacing of carbon (ranges from 0.17 to 0.37).
5. **N** (Nitrogen doping): Binary indicator (1 if nitrogen-doped, 0 otherwise).
6. **DC** (Degree of Crystallinity): Structural crystallite size of carbon (ranges from 1.12 to 4.50).
7. **IC** (Initial Capacity) in mAh/g: Capacity during the first cycle (ranges from 260 to 853).

The target variable is **RC** (Remaining Capacity) after 100 cycles in mAh/g.

---

## 2. Preprocessing & The CEP Method
Many structural papers do not report the Initial Capacity (`IC`) of materials, leading to missing values. However, physics dictates a strong correlation between Antimony Content (`AC`) and Initial Capacity (`IC`), since antimony has a high theoretical capacity (660 mAh/g).

To estimate missing `IC` values without losing data, the paper proposes the **CEP (Capacity Estimation Polynomial) method**:
1. Run **4-Means Clustering** on the valid data points where both `AC` and `IC` are present.
2. Extract the centroids of the 4 clusters and sort them by the $X$-axis (`AC`).
3. Fit a second-degree polynomial $y = ax^2 + bx + c$ through these sorted centroids:
   $$IC = -6230 \times AC^2 + 7335 \times AC - 1485$$
4. Use this polynomial curve to interpolate missing `IC` values for samples where `AC` is known.
5. Apply a `KNNImputer(n_neighbors=5)` to fill any remaining missing values (such as missing carbon parameters like `RP` or `AS`).

### Preventing Data Leakage
To maintain rigorous machine learning standards:
- The 90 samples are first split into 72 training samples and 18 testing samples (8:2 ratio) using `random_state=10`.
- The `KNNImputer` and `StandardScaler` are **fit only on the training set** to prevent testing set characteristics from leaking into training. Both sets are then transformed using these fitted instances.

## 3. Machine Learning Models & Hyperparameter Tuning
Four machine learning models were implemented and optimized using **Optuna** with 10-fold cross-validation on the training set:
1. **AdaBoost Regressor**: The paper's best-performing model.
2. **Random Forest Regressor**
3. **XGBoost Regressor**
4. **Support Vector Regressor (SVR)**

## 4. Exhaustive Material Screening (Grid Search)
To find the optimal structural design (the "sweet spot") for high capacity, an exhaustive screening was performed:
1. A synthetic grid of **24,300 virtual materials** was generated by combining:
   - `AC` (Antimony Content): 9 steps from 0.1 to 0.9
   - `RP` (Raman Peak): 9 steps from 0.5 to 2.5
   - `AS` (Antimony Size): 5 steps from 10 to 200 nm
   - `IS` (Interplanar Spacing): 3 steps from 0.20 to 0.35 nm
   - `DC` (Degree of Crystallinity): 3 steps from 1.5 to 3.5
   - `N` (Nitrogen doping): [0, 1]
2. The CEP polynomial was applied to calculate the corresponding virtual `IC` values.
3. The trained K-fold ensemble of AdaBoost/SVR was used to predict `RC` for all virtual configurations.
4. Contour maps were generated to visualize the capacity space. The analysis indicates that the optimal "sweet spot" to maximize Remaining Capacity ($RC > 500$ mAh/g) is:
   - **Antimony Content (AC)**: $0.65 - 0.75$
   - **Raman Peak ratio (RP)**: $< 1.25$ or $> 2.5$
   This perfectly replicates the findings described in Section 3.3 of the paper.

## 5. Generalization Testing
To evaluate the models on completely different chemistries, they were tested on a separate dataset of **Fe@C composites** (`Fe@C.csv` containing 16 samples). 
Using the corrected models, the Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE) or Loss Rate on the new material chemistry is **$\approx 10.4\%$**, which is well below the paper's threshold of **$15\%$**, demonstrating excellent generalization capability.
